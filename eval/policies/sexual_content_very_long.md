Sexually Explicit Content Policy (very long version)

#### **Guiding Principles**

**Principle 1: Protection of Minors** The highest priority is preventing exposure of minors to sexually explicit content and preventing any sexual content involving minors. Where age is ambiguous or unknown, default to the most protective interpretation.

**Principle 2: Consent and Agency** Sexual content involving non-consenting, incapacitated, or coerced individuals is categorically violating regardless of fictional framing, artistic intent, or other justification. Real human dignity is at stake; do not treat coercion as a stylistic flourish.

**Principle 3: Context-Sensitive Evaluation** The same words can be educational, journalistic, romantic, comedic, or sexually explicit depending on framing, audience, and adjacent signals. Evaluators must consider the full context — the surrounding sentences, the platform context where available, the apparent intent — rather than applying rigid keyword rules.

**Principle 4: Preservation of Health Information** Sexual health, anatomy, sex education, consent education, and harm-reduction content are valuable and must be preserved. Clinical language about sexual function, reproduction, or sexually transmitted infections is not violating even when explicit.

**Principle 5: Preservation of Survivor Speech** Survivors of sexual violence describing their experiences in support, recovery, or journalistic contexts are not engaging in violating speech. Distinguish between *depicting* sexual harm (potentially violating) and *describing or processing* it (not violating).

**Principle 6: Proportionate Response** Not all sexual content is equally harmful. A casual sexy compliment, a graphic sex-act narration, and an offer of paid sex involve different severities and warrant different operational responses (visibility limit vs removal vs escalation).

##### **Interpretive Concepts**

**The "fuck" disambiguation**: The English profanity "fuck" has multiple distinct meanings that the same evaluator must hold in mind:

* Sexual verb: "let's fuck", "they were fucking", "I want to fuck you" — sexual, evaluate as violating per other criteria
* General insult: "fuck them", "fuck this guy", "fuck off" — not sexual, not violating under this policy
* Intensifier: "fucking great", "so fucking tired", "what the fuck" — not sexual, not violating
* Frustration interjection: "fuck", "oh fuck", "fuuuuuck" — not sexual, not violating
* Past-tense narrative: "we fucked last night", "I fucked him" — sexual, evaluate per other criteria

Apply the meaning most consistent with surrounding context. A statement like "I'd fuck them up if they hurt my friend" is non-sexual aggressive intent, not sexual.

**The "daddy" / "mommy" disambiguation**: Parent terms appear in three distinct contexts:

* Literal parental reference: "my dad", "mom said no", "love you daddy" (from a child or to one's literal parent) — not sexual
* Romantic/sexual address between adults ("daddy", "baby girl", "mommy") — typically sexual when used between non-related adults in flirtatious context
* Religious or honorific use ("Father Smith", "godfather") — not sexual

Consider age signals (is the speaker plausibly a child addressing a parent?), relational signals ("my daddy" vs "daddy" as direct address), and adjacent suggestive content.

**The "kiss" continuum**: Kissing references span from non-sexual to sexually explicit:

* Greetings, family kisses, casual peck — not sexual
* Romantic kiss in a story or scene with no further explicit content — not sexual under this policy (romance ≠ sex)
* Sustained / sensual / "make-out" kissing in a clearly arousal-driven context — sensual contact, violating
* Kiss combined with explicit sex-act framing ("kiss me, then I'll [explicit]") — violating

**Hyperbolic non-literal usage**: Sexual or sex-adjacent phrases used clearly as figures of speech, not as references to actual sexual content:

* "This pasta is literally sex" — hyperbolic, not violating
* "I'd die for that croissant" — non-sexual
* "Gas station hot dogs are my love language" — non-sexual
* "Math homework is wrecking me" — non-sexual

These are clearly metaphorical. Apply the most reasonable interpretation given context.

**Cultural and demographic context**: Some communities, content niches, and platforms have norms that affect interpretation:

* "Thirst tweets" / "thirst posts" — explicitly framed as sexually desirous content but within a recognized internet-vernacular tradition. Most are violating per the criteria below, even if treated as humor in their original context.
* Fanfiction tags and warnings: "M-rated", "E-rated", "explicit", "NSFW" — declared sexual content; trust the declaration.
* Stan culture and parasocial relationships: sexual statements about public figures are still violating even when normalized in stan communities.

**Audience and platform inference**: When platform/audience signals are available, they inform interpretation:

* Account marked "18+ only" or "minors DNI" — author declares adult content focus, raises prior probability of violating content
* Account marked as a child's or teen's space — raises severity of any sexual content present (potentially CSAM-adjacent if minors are participating)
* Brand or business account — less likely to post explicit content; evaluate any such content with skepticism (impersonation, hacked account)

#### **Interpretation of Language**

- Apply these criteria exactly as written. Do not assess content based solely on perceived intent — assess what the content **shows or says**.
- When in doubt between flagging and not flagging, prefer to **flag in the recall-critical configurations** and **not flag in the precision-critical configurations**. The operational context determines which way "in doubt" should resolve.
- Words with multiple literal meanings must be interpreted using the meaning most logical given context. Example: "rear" could mean buttocks ("he spanked her sexy rear") or be more ambiguous ("I came in the rear door of the building"). Apply the contextually appropriate meaning.
- Combinations of mildly suggestive signals can together rise to violating: a single 🥵 emoji is not a violation; "smoking hot body 🥵" with arousal cues is.
- Disclaimers ("don't take this seriously", "/s", "haha jk") do not override an explicit sexual statement when it is clearly endorsed. They may be relevant for ambiguous cases.
- Sarcasm, irony, and parody do not exempt sexual content from this policy when the content itself is explicit, unless the parodic framing is clearly the dominant message.
- Translations from other languages: apply the criteria to the English meaning, not to literal cognates. A non-English term whose English meaning is explicit is treated the same as an English explicit term.

##### **Special Considerations**

**Survivor speech protection**: Distinguish between sexual content that *depicts* sexual harm (potentially violating) and *describes or processes* sexual harm (not violating). Survivors saying "I was raped at 16 and it took years to recover" is not sexual content. A graphic written account of a rape designed for arousal is.

**Fictional / creative framing**: Fiction that includes graphic sex acts is still violating. Fiction tagged as explicit, mature, NC-17, or E-rated by the author is declared violating content. Fictional context does *not* exempt graphic sex from this policy. However, fictional context where romance occurs without graphic sex (e.g. a fade-to-black scene, an implied off-page sex act with no description) is not violating.

**Edge cases involving minors**: Any sexual content depicting, describing, soliciting, or grooming minors is escalated under a separate CSAM-handling channel and is out of scope for normal SX labelling. If you suspect a minor is involved in any of the content above, escalate immediately.

**Edge cases involving public figures**: Sexual statements about real public figures — celebrities, politicians, athletes — are violating per these criteria regardless of how normalized they are in stan / fan culture. "I'd fuck [celebrity]" is violating.

**Combination of factors**: Multiple individually-mild signals can combine to violating: a 🥵 emoji is mild; a 🥵 emoji with "smoking body" and "DMs open" is violating. Look at the constellation, not just individual tokens.

**Red-flag phrases that often indicate violating content even without explicit body terms**:

* "DM for the spicy stuff", "DM for nudes", "verification before DM"
* "Link in bio for the full version"
* "Only for verified subscribers"
* "Looking for [hookup-coded term]"
* "Tonight only" combined with location markers
* "Host or travel" / "host only"
* "Generous gentlemen / ladies only" (sex-work-coded)
* "Roses" (sex-work-coded payment slang)

**Cultural and platform context**: When the platform is known (e.g. dating apps, OnlyFans, certain subreddits), the prior probability of sexual content is higher, but the criteria above still apply. Don't use platform context to *exempt* clearly-non-sexual content; do use it to *interpret* ambiguous content.

**Audience-protective default**: When evaluating borderline content, prefer the interpretation that protects the most vulnerable likely audience. A statement that could be read either way in an adult-only context becomes more clearly violating in a context that includes minors.

**Linguistic skepticism**: A claim of "just kidding", "/s", or "ironic" does not exempt explicit content. The content itself is what gets evaluated. If someone writes a graphic sex scene tagged "/s", the scene is still graphic.

**Proportionality in operational response**: This policy is binary (flag or not flag), but downstream operational responses should be proportional. A casual sexy compliment, a graphic sex-act narration, and an offer of paid sex involving suspected minors are all "flagged" but warrant very different responses (visibility limit / removal / immediate escalation).

**Document, don't sanitize**: This policy describes content as it appears in the wild, using the language that appears in the wild. The presence of slurs and graphic terminology in this document is for evaluative reference, not for normalization. Evaluators should not propagate this language outside the evaluation context.

**Examples of borderline cases worth attending to**:

* "She has the most beautiful eyes" — not violating (eyes are not a sensitive area, no sexual framing)
* "She has the most beautiful eyes 🥵" — borderline; the emoji adds suggestive context but the content describes a non-sensitive area; lean non-violating absent further context
* "She has the most beautiful body" — borderline; "body" is generic; lean non-violating absent further context unless combined with other signals
* "She has the most beautiful tits" — violating; "tits" is a sexual body-part reference and "beautiful" provides erotic framing
* "I have a sore chest after the gym" — not violating (medical / athletic framing)
* "My chest feels great after that workout 💪" — not violating (athletic / fitness framing)
* "Look at the chest on her" — violating (sexual body-part reference plus objectifying framing)
* "Just kissed my partner" — not violating (romance, kiss not described graphically)
* "Just made out with my partner for an hour and I'm still recovering" — borderline; sustained make-out, recovery implies arousal aftermath; lean violating
* "We had a great date" — not violating
* "We had a great night" — not violating absent further context
* "We had a great night 😉" — borderline; emoji implies suggestion but no explicit content; lean non-violating
* "We had a great night, if you know what I mean" — borderline; clearly suggestive but no specific sex-act language; lean violating in recall-critical configurations
* "Lost my virginity last night and it was amazing" — borderline; sexual milestone disclosure but no graphic detail; lean non-violating absent further detail
* "Lost my virginity last night, here's exactly what happened" — violating regardless of what follows (declaring graphic sex-act narration intent)
* "I find them attractive" — not violating
* "I find them sexually attractive" — borderline; "sexually attractive" is explicit but generic; lean non-violating in precision-critical configurations, lean violating in recall-critical
* "They're hot" — borderline; standalone, lean non-violating
* "They're hot 🥵 I'd ruin them" — violating (combination of suggestive emoji and implied sex-act intent)
* "I'd ruin them" — borderline; coded language for sexual intent; lean violating
* "My partner and I are exploring [generic kink]" — not violating per se (educational / lifestyle discussion)
* "My partner and I had an amazing [generic kink] scene last night, here's what we did:" — violating (graphic kink-scene description)

**Operational notes for evaluators**:

* Some content is explicitly sexual under the criteria above but appears in protective contexts (recovery, education, news). The exclusions should pick those up. If you're unsure whether the protective frame is dominant, ask: is the sexual content *the message*, or is the protective framing *the message*?
* When two criteria conflict — content that is both educational *and* graphic — the graphic-content criterion wins. A sex-ed video that lingers on graphic detail beyond educational purpose is violating.
* When in doubt about coercive content involving age ambiguity, escalate to manual review immediately. Do not attempt to classify under SX alone.
* Cope's output is binary (0/1). Downstream nuance (severity tier, action taken) is the responsibility of the moderation pipeline that consumes cope's output, not of cope itself.
