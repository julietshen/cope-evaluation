Self-Harm Policy

# Criteria

## Overview

This policy provides criteria for identifying content that promotes, instructs, glorifies, recruits for, or otherwise enables suicide and self-harm.

## Definition of Terms

- **Self-Harm**: Intentional, self-directed injury to one's own body. Includes cutting, burning, scratching, hitting, biting, ingesting harmful substances, and any other act intended to cause physical injury, with or without suicidal intent.
- **Suicide**: The act of intentionally ending one's own life.
- **Suicidal Ideation**: Thoughts about, consideration of, or desire to end one's life. Includes:
  - **Active Ideation**: Direct statements of intent, planning, or desire to die.
  - **Passive Ideation**: Indirect expressions such as wishing not to wake up, wanting to disappear, or wishing for external events to end one's life.
- **Methods, Means, and Tools**: Specific techniques, instruments, substances, locations, products, or behaviors used to carry out self-harm or suicide. Examples include sharp implements, ligatures, firearms, medications, jumping points, and concealment products.
- **Glorification**: Presenting self-harm or suicide in aesthetic, romantic, heroic, artistic, or aspirational terms, including imagery, "thinspiration"-style collections, and content framing scars, wounds, or tools as desirable or beautiful.
- **Recruitment**: Inviting, soliciting, or organizing others to participate in self-harm or suicide. Includes pacts, accountability buddies for harming, group challenges, competitions, and counts.
- **Detection Avoidance**: Techniques, products, or advice intended to hide self-harm from family members, clinicians, teachers, or other observers (e.g., concealing wounds with makeup, choosing methods that don't leave marks).
- **Risk Mitigation**: Harm-reduction strategies, recovery support, treatment information, and crisis resources intended to reduce the harm associated with self-harm or suicide.
- **Explicit Portrayals**: Detailed descriptions of self-harm acts, wounds, methods, or effects presented in specific and graphic terms.

## Interpretation of Language

- Apply these criteria exactly as written.
- Do not assess content based on implications except where specifically instructed.
- When in doubt between flagging and not flagging, prefer to flag — false negatives in this domain carry greater harm than false positives.
- Statements should be interpreted in their plain meaning. Sarcasm, irony, or "/s" markers should not override an explicit endorsement.

## Definition of Label

### (SELF_HARM) Self-Harm

#### Includes

- **Content Providing Methods or Instructions for Self-Harm**: Content that supplies, requests, or offers to share methods, techniques, tools, products, or instructions for self-harming or attempting suicide:
  - **Method Sharing**: Describing specific techniques, tools, substances, or behaviors that cause self-injury.
  - **Instruction Requests**: Asking how to self-harm, which tool to use, how deep to cut, what dosage, etc.
  - **Tool Marketing or Distribution**: Selling, trading, advertising, or directing others to physical items primarily used for self-harm; sharing "collections" of such items.
  - **Concealment Techniques**: Methods, products, or advice for hiding self-harm from observers (e.g., makeup tutorials for fresh wounds, methods that "don't leave marks").

- **Content Expressing Suicidal Ideation, Intent, or Plans**: Content where the author expresses or describes their own ideation or intent:
  - **Active Statements**: "I'm going to kill myself", "I want to die", "I've decided to end it".
  - **Plans and Preparations**: Specifying a time, place, method, or means; writing goodbye letters; acquiring means; visiting a chosen location.
  - **Conditional Statements**: "If X happens, I'll kill myself" or otherwise tying the act to a future trigger.
  - **Passive Ideation**: Wishing not to wake up, wanting to disappear, wanting external events to end one's life — even when no explicit method is named.
  - **Continued or Escalating Self-Harm**: Statements of current self-harm, urges, or intent to escalate ("thinking of adding more tonight", "deeper this time").

- **Content Glorifying, Romanticizing, or Promoting Self-Harm**: Content that frames self-harm or suicide in positive, beautiful, or aspirational terms:
  - **Aesthetic Framing**: "Aesthetic" boards, "art" framings, treating scars or wounds as beautiful or as trophies.
  - **Endorsement as Coping**: Framing self-harm as the best coping mechanism, better than alternatives, or as something the audience is "missing out on".
  - **Romanticization of Pain**: Framing pain, scars, or suicidal feelings as poetic, deep, more real than ordinary life.
  - **Aspirational Comparisons**: Encouraging others to go "deeper", count higher, or otherwise compete.

- **Content Encouraging or Recruiting Others to Self-Harm**: Content directed at others rather than the author's own state:
  - **Direct Encouragement**: Telling others they should harm themselves, suggesting they go further, validating their urges to harm.
  - **Recruitment**: Soliciting partners, accountability buddies for self-harm, joint pacts, or scheduled group sessions.
  - **Challenges and Competitions**: Numeric counts, leaderboards, "who can go deepest", rewards for participation.
  - **Communities of Practice**: Inviting others to private groups, Discord servers, or channels that center on self-harming behavior.

- **Content Sharing Self-Harm Imagery, Counts, or Trophies**: Content where the author shares evidence of self-harm in a non-clinical, non-recovery framing:
  - **Wound or Scar Display**: Posting images, descriptions, or counts of wounds or scars in a non-medical context.
  - **Tool or "Collection" Display**: Posting images or descriptions of tools used for self-harm.
  - **Progress Tracking**: Documenting frequency, depth, or escalation of self-harm.

- **Content Facilitating Access to Suicide Means**: Content where the author seeks to acquire, ranks, or directs others to means of suicide:
  - **Means Research**: Ranking or researching methods for lethality or painlessness.
  - **Acquisition**: Acquiring quantities of medications, weapons, or other means specifically for self-harm.
  - **Location Targeting**: Identifying or visiting specific locations chosen for suicide.

#### Excludes

- **Recovery and Harm-Reduction Content**: Content focused on healing, recovery, or reducing harm:
  - **Personal Recovery Narratives**: Past self-harm described as resolved, in remission, or in active treatment, without current urges and without method instructions.
  - **Support Resources**: Referring to or sharing crisis lines, treatment options, peer support, and clinical resources.
  - **Harm Reduction**: Discussion of safer alternatives, wound care, and risk-reduction strategies in a clinical or recovery context.
  - **Requirement**: Must not include explicit portrayals, glorification, or method instructions.

- **Educational, Journalistic, and Prevention Content**: Objective discussion of self-harm and suicide:
  - **Academic and Clinical**: Research, statistics, public-health information, clinical descriptions for training purposes.
  - **Journalism**: News coverage of suicides, trends, or related issues, presented neutrally and consistent with safe-messaging guidelines.
  - **Prevention**: Warning-sign education, how to support someone in crisis, intervention training.
  - **Requirement**: Must not include detailed method instructions, glorification, or encouragement.

- **Fictional and Creative Content (Narrowly)**: Fictional portrayals of self-harm in clearly creative contexts:
  - **Creative Works**: Self-harm depicted as part of a fictional narrative, without method instructions or promotional framing.
  - **Exclusion**: Does not include content that uses fictional framing as a shield to deliver method instructions or to glorify.

- **General Distress Without Self-Harm Content**: Expressions of emotional difficulty that do not reference self-harm:
  - **Sadness, Stress, Burnout, Depression**: Personal expressions of low mood, fatigue, or distress without mention of self-injury or suicidal ideation.
  - **Explicit Disavowal**: Content where the author explicitly states they are not suicidal and not self-harming, even when discussing difficult feelings.

- **Resolved Past Behavior**: Self-harm referenced strictly as a past, resolved event:
  - **Requirement**: No current urges, no method content, no encouragement of others.

- **Refusals**: Content where the author refuses to provide self-harm instructions, methods, or encouragement, or declines to participate in self-harm activities.
